# Worked Examples: Bulletproof Problem Solving

All examples are paraphrased and compressed for methodology and eval use.

## Sydney Airport Capacity

Problem: determine whether Sydney Airport passenger capacity will be adequate in the future.

Expert logic:

- Bound the problem around passenger airport capacity, excluding broader policy debates unless needed.
- First cut: supply of landing/passenger capacity versus demand for passenger movements.
- Supply can be decomposed into number of runways, capacity per runway, and utilization.
- Demand can be decomposed into regional demand growth and Sydney's share of that demand.
- Productive depth focuses on runway utilization because it is one of the few manageable levers: operating hours, movements per hour, and people per aircraft.
- A strong candidate identifies what can be changed, such as aircraft spacing, slot pricing, peak light-aircraft policy, and aircraft size.

Skill implication: use mathematical or operational driver trees when the problem is a capacity adequacy question. Depth should concentrate on controllable bottlenecks.

## Residential Solar Decision

Problem: decide whether to install home solar panels now.

Expert logic:

- Hypothesis: install now.
- Branches are conditions that must hold for the hypothesis: investment payback is attractive, waiting is not better because cost declines are insufficient, and carbon reduction is material.
- Payback dominates if economics are decisive, but nonfinancial objectives may keep carbon impact in scope.
- Waiting analysis compares lost savings during the delay against possible future installation-cost decline.
- Risks include subsidy changes and feed-in tariff reductions.

Skill implication: a personal or investment decision can use an AND/constraint tree where failure of one branch can defeat the recommendation, unless the decision maker has multiple weighted objectives.

## Where Should I Move?

Problem: choose a town for a family move.

Expert logic:

- Define what the decision maker values: schools, natural environment, recreation, town quality, and ability to earn income.
- Break each value into measurable indicators.
- Weight variables based on family priorities.
- Prune variables that do not discriminate among serious options or can be collapsed into a better measure.
- Normalize indicators and compare candidate locations.
- Sensitivity matters because subjective weights can shift the ranking.

Skill implication: option-selection trees often need weighted criteria rather than strict AND logic. Full tree can be broad, while the pruned tree should keep differentiating variables.

## Start-Up Pricing Decision

Problem: decide whether a young company should raise prices despite demand risk.

Expert logic:

- Use a profit lever tree because the decision is mathematically tied to price, variable margin, unit volume, and cash generation.
- The issue is not just whether price can rise, but whether higher price improves contribution after potential volume loss and customer reaction.
- Scenario analysis can compare price lift, demand elasticity, channel response, and cash needs.

Skill implication: if a problem has a complete financial equation, build the equation first and put behavioral uncertainties beneath the variables.

## Pacific Salmon Conservation

Problem: identify conservation strategy for Pacific salmon abundance and diversity.

Expert logic:

- First-cut component tree maps broad levers: freshwater habitat, ocean conditions, fishing pressure, hatcheries/farms, consumer behavior, and policy.
- The first tree is useful but imperfect: cross-cutting policy was misplaced as a separate branch, regional differences were hidden, and branch magnitudes were unclear.
- The tree evolves toward hypotheses about where intervention can matter.
- Pruning uses impact and ability to influence. Some major drivers are deprioritized because they are difficult to affect; some lower-impact levers are deferred because they are politically hard or less cost-effective.

Skill implication: for complex systems, the skill should show a full systems map, call out cross-cutting forces, then produce a pruned intervention tree.

## Contested Historical Legacies

Problem: develop a principled way to handle monuments or artifacts tied to morally contested historical figures.

Expert logic:

- Use inductive reasoning from many cases to generate threshold questions.
- Reorder the threshold questions into a decision tree.
- Decision tree guides sanctions or responses more consistently than ad hoc reactions.

Skill implication: when principles are unclear, work inductively from examples before imposing a decision hierarchy.

## Climate Abatement Cost Curve

Problem: identify useful ways to reduce emissions.

Expert logic:

- Common frames include mitigation/adaptation and supply/demand.
- A cost-curve frame reveals abatement activities by cost and tons reduced.
- The frame exposes no-regrets or positive-return actions that can be missed by individual footprint or generic policy debates.

Skill implication: choose the cleaving frame that changes what actions become visible.

## Asthma Incidence/Severity

Problem: understand urban asthma differences and possible interventions.

Expert logic:

- The incidence/severity cut reveals that incidence differences may be modest while hospitalization and death differences are much larger.
- This points to severity drivers such as particulate matter, tree cover, and socioeconomic conditions.
- The cut leads to a more targeted intervention hypothesis than a generic public-health tree.

Skill implication: incidence/severity is a powerful non-business axis for health, safety, quality, and reliability problems.

## Long-Term Resource Investment

Problem: value a long-lived mining asset under uncertainty.

Expert logic:

- Base valuation tree estimates cash flows from the existing asset.
- A separate branch captures development options created by infrastructure.
- Major uncertainties include commodity price, exchange rate, production volume, and option value.
- Scenario and payoff tables make downside and upside combinations visible.

Skill implication: long-duration investments require explicit option value and uncertainty branches, not only base-case economics.

## Strategic Staircases

Problem: build a new business under uncertainty.

Expert logic:

- Work backward from the target state to required capabilities, assets, and learning.
- Sequence steps that buy information, build capability, and expand commitment as uncertainty falls.
- Evaluate stretch, momentum, and flexibility.

Skill implication: strategy under uncertainty can be decomposed as a staged learning and capability tree.

# Surprise Register

## 1. Cleaving quality is the core skill

The book's highest-value idea is not that trees should be MECE. It is that the cut through the problem determines whether insight is visible. Supply/demand, incidence/severity, and impact/influenceability can make different solution paths appear.

## 2. First trees are allowed to be weak if they are used honestly

The salmon case treats the first component tree as useful but flawed. The problem is not having a rough first tree; the problem is mistaking it for the final answer.

## 3. Cross-cutting forces often break naive MECE

Government policy in the salmon tree is a cross-cutting force that affects many levers. LLM trees often put such forces as a sibling branch, which creates overlap and hides mechanism.

## 4. Pruning is a positive act, not incompleteness

The book repeatedly separates the full logical universe from what is worth working on. A strong skill should make the excluded branches visible and explain why they are excluded.

## 5. Simple arithmetic can validate a frame

The solar and asthma examples show that back-of-the-envelope calculations can prove whether a decomposition axis is useful before deeper research begins.

## 6. Non-business frames transfer into business and vice versa

Policy frames such as regulate/incent or mitigate/adapt can clarify business questions; business frames such as options and cost curves can clarify social questions.

## 7. The tree can become the story, but only after synthesis

The final story is a pyramid or decision path built from evidence, not the raw workplan. This supports separate full tree, pruned workplan, and final answer structures.

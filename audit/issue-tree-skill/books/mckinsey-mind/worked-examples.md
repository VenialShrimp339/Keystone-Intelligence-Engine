# Worked Examples: The McKinsey Mind

All examples are paraphrased and compressed for methodology and eval use.

## Acme Widgets Profit Logic Tree

Problem: increase profits for a multi-business company.

Expert logic:

- Start with the profit source question.
- Break profit by business unit or function, depending on what view reveals the issue.
- Under each unit, split revenue and expenses, then continue to drivers.
- The chosen top-level cut affects what issues become visible, so the team should consider alternative cuts.

Skill implication: a logic tree can be structurally MECE while still choosing a weak top-level axis. Axis selection must be explicit.

## Customer Information Business Case

Problem: justify investment in customer information management.

Expert logic:

- Decompose value sources: more products sold to existing customers, lower service costs, lower attrition, and more efficient new-customer acquisition.
- For each value source, estimate expected economic benefit and cost.
- Build the business case by reconstructing where value comes from.

Skill implication: good trees expose value pools and link each branch to a quantifiable benefit.

## Federal State of the Union Pillars

Problem: organize a public-policy agenda for a presidential address.

Expert logic:

- Frame each policy area with problem, dimensions, intended action, resources, feasibility, and political implications.
- Use a consistent framework across heterogeneous issues so leadership can respond.

Skill implication: broad public-sector decomposition must include achievability, resources, and political constraints, not just substantive policy analysis.

## Acme Thrum-Mat Hypothesis

Problem: lower marginal cost for a manufacturing product.

Expert logic:

- Generate options: lower raw-material cost, cut labor, shorten curing process.
- Use QDT to test each: raw materials matter but supplier leverage is weak; labor matters but productivity is already strong; shorter curing may reduce cost and working capital if quality holds.
- Build an issue tree for the surviving hypothesis.
- Top issues: cost reduction, implementation feasibility, quality maintenance.
- Under implementation, ask whether special facilities or special skills are required; if yes, analyze whether they can be acquired.

Skill implication: tree depth follows conditional logic. Some answers close branches; others create follow-on questions.

## Conseco E-Business Strategy

Problem: prioritize many possible e-business initiatives.

Expert logic:

- Start broad enough to be MECE, but do not investigate every possibility equally.
- Identify key levers of profitability and growth.
- Prune ideas that cannot materially move economics.

Skill implication: full tree and pruned tree are both necessary. Completeness and equal investigation are different standards.

## HSDD Market Sizing

Problem: estimate market potential for a not-yet-established condition and product.

Expert logic:

- Direct data is limited or absent.
- Use analogies such as adjacent sexual-health products and lifestyle/mental-health conditions.
- Triangulate patient recognition, doctor diagnosis, willingness to discuss, treatment seeking, and adoption.
- Bound the answer rather than pretending precision.

Skill implication: when direct facts are missing, leaves should define bounding analyses and analogical evidence.

## Presentation From Issue Tree

Problem: present recommendation after analysis.

Expert logic:

- The hypothesis and major issue-tree branches become the top slide or governing argument.
- Supporting sections follow the branch structure.
- If the argument cannot be explained quickly, the underlying issue tree or hypothesis is probably unclear.

Skill implication: the skill should include an executive-summary or elevator-test check as part of quality validation.

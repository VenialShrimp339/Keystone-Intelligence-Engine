# Squarespace Growth Quality Branch Report

## Branch Prompt

Assess whether Squarespace's post-take-private growth base is strong enough for a PE sponsor to underwrite value creation without relying mainly on multiple expansion.

## Findings

- Permira completed the acquisition of Squarespace on October 17, 2024, and the transaction valued the company at approximately $7.2 billion, so the diligence question is now a sponsor-owned value-creation question rather than a public-shareholder voting question [SRC1].
- Squarespace's Q2 2024 total revenue was $296.8 million, up 20% year over year, which indicates the company entered the take-private with real top-line momentum rather than a stalled revenue base [SRC2].
- Annual run-rate revenue reached $1.179 billion in Q2 2024, up 20% year over year, while unique subscriptions increased 21% to more than 5.2 million, giving the sponsor a large subscription base to monetize [SRC2].
- Average revenue per unique subscription rose only 3% year over year to $225.45, which suggests that near-term value creation probably needs price-packaging, attachment, or mix improvements in addition to continued subscription growth [SRC2].
- Squarespace's own metric definition says ARRR is a run-rate indicator and not a GAAP revenue forecast, so underwriting should avoid treating ARRR as guaranteed next-year revenue [SRC3].
- Wix reported Q2 2024 bookings of $458.4 million, up 15% year over year, and attributed momentum partly to Wix Studio, AI capabilities, and commerce expansion, creating a competitive benchmark that Squarespace must answer with product depth and partner-led growth rather than brand alone [SRC4].
- The merger announcement warned of business disruption, employee-retention, financing, regulatory, and competitive-response risks, making continuity and execution quality explicit diligence gates after close [SRC5].

## Branch Assessment

The growth branch is directionally attractive but not self-proving. Subscription count and ARRR growth support further diligence, while modest ARPUS growth and Wix's stronger agency/Studio narrative imply that sponsor upside needs a focused monetization and product-extension plan. The most important disconfirming evidence would be cohort-level data showing that incremental subscriptions came from lower-quality Google Domains migration effects or low-retention price-sensitive customers.

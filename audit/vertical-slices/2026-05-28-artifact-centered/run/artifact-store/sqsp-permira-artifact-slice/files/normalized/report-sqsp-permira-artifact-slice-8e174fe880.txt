# Squarespace Margin And Cash Flow Branch Report

## Branch Prompt

Assess whether Squarespace's margin, cash-flow, and portfolio posture can support private-equity value creation after the Permira take-private.

## Findings

- Squarespace generated $72.1 million of adjusted EBITDA in Q2 2024 against $296.8 million of revenue, but adjusted EBITDA declined slightly year over year, so growth was not translating into automatic EBITDA expansion at the latest public checkpoint [SRC1].
- Unlevered free cash flow was $65.4 million in Q2 2024 and $154.7 million for the first half of 2024, giving the sponsor a cash-generative asset base for deleveraging, reinvestment, or tuck-in work [SRC1].
- Net cash provided by operating activities was $145.8 million for the first half of 2024 versus $116.7 million in the prior-year period, supporting the view that the company had improving cash conversion before delisting [SRC1].
- Squarespace's Q2 2024 disclosure warns that adjusted EBITDA and unlevered free cash flow are non-GAAP measures and should be used only alongside GAAP results, so diligence should reconcile reported non-GAAP cash generation to debt capacity and sponsor-case liquidity [SRC2].
- Squarespace agreed in June 2024 to sell Tock to American Express for $400 million in cash, creating a portfolio-simplification and liquidity event that can sharpen focus on the core website, domains, commerce, and entrepreneur platform [SRC3].
- American Express completed the Tock acquisition on October 15, 2024, removing a non-core hospitality platform from the Squarespace perimeter immediately before the Permira closing [SRC4].
- The transaction announcement's risk factors include financing, rating-agency, macro, litigation, disruption, and employee-retention risks, so the cash-flow branch should include downside cases for debt cost, retention, and execution disruption rather than assuming a frictionless take-private [SRC5].

## Branch Assessment

The margin and cash-flow branch supports proceeding to deeper diligence, with a caution. Squarespace had meaningful unlevered free cash flow and a cleaner portfolio after Tock, but the public checkpoint did not show clear EBITDA expansion. The next analytical artifact should be a sponsor-case bridge that separates recurring subscription gross margin, operating expense leverage, Google Domains integration effects, post-Tock perimeter changes, debt service, and reinvestment needs.

# Archetypes

These are examples and priors. Adapt the branch logic to the problem.

## Market Sizing

Use multiple bounding paths:

- top-down: population or accounts x eligibility x adoption x usage x price
- bottom-up: customers x units x frequency x price
- supply constraint: capacity x utilization x throughput
- analogs: adjacent markets, competitor revenue, channel data

Grade by decision accuracy, not false precision.

## Market Entry

Candidate branches:

- market attractiveness
- right to win
- economics and investment case
- execution feasibility
- risks and constraints
- strategic fit and exit/options

Label branches as weighted factors or constraint gates.

## Profitability Diagnostic

Start with the equation:

- revenue: price, volume, mix, retention, share
- cost: variable, fixed, overhead, productivity, inputs
- capital/assets: utilization, working capital, invested capital

Then place causes beneath the affected driver.

## Pricing

Candidate branches:

- willingness to pay and value created
- volume/elasticity risk
- competitive response
- margin and cash impact
- channel/contract constraints
- implementation and communication

## M&A / Diligence

Candidate branches:

- investment thesis
- market attractiveness
- target quality
- standalone economics
- value creation or synergy
- risks/liabilities
- deal feasibility
- exit/downside protection

State what would kill the deal early.

## Operations Diagnostic

Candidate branches:

- arrival demand
- process flow
- bottlenecks
- capacity/staffing
- quality/rework
- downstream constraints
- measurement artifacts

## Technical Architecture

Candidate branches:

- current failure diagnosis
- requirements and constraints
- option set
- trade-offs: reliability, velocity, security, cost, maintainability
- migration feasibility
- team capability

Do not recommend architecture change until diagnosis supports it.

## Policy

Candidate branches:

- objectives and target population
- mechanism of change
- fiscal cost
- distributional effects
- administrative feasibility
- legal/regulatory constraints
- political feasibility
- externalities and unintended consequences

## Scientific Evidence

Candidate branches:

- prior evidence strength
- causal or biological plausibility
- study quality and bias
- reproducibility and external validity
- feasibility of decisive research
- expected value of information
- opportunity cost
